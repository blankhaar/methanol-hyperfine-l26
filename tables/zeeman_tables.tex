\section{Hyperfine-resolved Zeeman coefficients}\label{sec:sm-zeeman}\label{sec:supp-zeeman}
\subsection{Transition set and magnetic inputs}
Tables~\ref{tab:sm-zeeman-6p18}--\ref{tab:sm-zeeman-107} give
coefficients for 27 methanol maser transitions, including those studied
by Lankhaar et al.\cite{Lankhaar2018Zeeman} and
Dall'Olio et al.\cite{DallOlio2020}
The set includes both $A$ and $E$ symmetry and three $v_t=1$ transitions.
Each table lists the eight
components with the largest Einstein $A$ coefficients.
The electronic catalogue in \path{supplement/zeeman/} contains all 750 nonzero hyperfine components. The calculation code and versioned inputs are publicly available in the repository \href{https://github.com/blankhaar/methanol-hyperfine-l26}{methanol-hyperfine-l26}.

The magnetic inputs are $G_{aa}^{(0)}=0.305$, $G_{bb}^{(0)}=-0.060$, and $G_{cc}^{(0)}=-0.038$ from Takagi et al., with their effective axial input $-f b'_a=g_x=0.425$, $f=5.23461381$, $b'_b=b'_c=0$, and $g_p=5.5856946893$.\cite{Takagi2021} Unconstrained off-diagonal and $n=3,6$ rotational terms retain the calculated values of Lankhaar et al.\cite{Lankhaar2018Zeeman} This prescription uses the adopted principal-axis/RAM alignment approximation and Takagi's assumed absolute sign; it does not propagate a calibrated magnetic uncertainty.

The dimensionless level factors obey $\Delta E/h=-(\mu_N/h)g_FM_FB$, with $g_1=g_u$ and $g_2=g_l$. The transition coefficient $z$ is the full signed $q=+1$ minus $q=-1$ strength-weighted centroid separation per unit field, where $q=M_u-M_l$. An observational RCP/LCP sign requires a specified polarization convention.

Within each rotational level, subscripts $a,b,c,d$ distinguish
hyperfine eigenstates of the same $F$ in increasing energy order.
The offsets $\Delta\nu$ are measured from the complete
statistical-strength centroid.
Nominal frequencies entering the $\nu^3$ factor in $A$ follow
M\"uller et al.,\cite{Mueller2004} Breen et al.,\cite{Breen2019ZeemanSM}
and MacLeod et al.\cite{MacLeod2019ZeemanSM}
The 38.293-GHz line is assigned $A^-$ and the 38.453-GHz line $A^+$,
consistent with the level energies and Breen et al.; the two labels
in M\"uller et al. are reversed.
% Per-transition nominal frequencies follow the literature sources listed above.
These literature values supply the nominal frequency in the Einstein-$A$ calculation; the electronic transition summary retains their individual provenance. The hyperfine offsets are not absolute corrections to catalogue rest frequencies.
Dipole matrix elements use the torsion--rotation wavefunctions and
$\mu_a=0.899$~D, $\mu_b=-1.44$~D, and $\mu_c=0$.
We define the dimensionless reduced hyperfine strength by
\begin{equation}
s_{ul}=\frac{|\langle uF_u\Vert\mu^{(1)}\Vert lF_l\rangle|^2}{|d_{\rm tr}|^2},
\end{equation}
where $d_{\rm tr}$ is the reduced torsion--rotation dipole matrix element before coupling to nuclear spin. The hyperfine eigenstates include the spin mixing in the L26 Hamiltonian. Our Wigner--Eckart convention is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\langle uF_uM_u|\mu^{(1)}_q|lF_lM_l\rangle
={}&(-1)^{F_u-M_u}
\begin{pmatrix}F_u&1&F_l\\-M_u&q&M_l\end{pmatrix}\\
&\times\langle uF_u\Vert\mu^{(1)}\Vert lF_l\rangle .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The Einstein coefficient is then
\begin{equation}
A_{ul}=A_{\rm rot}\frac{2J_u+1}{2F_u+1}s_{ul},
\qquad \sum_l A_{ul}=A_{\rm rot}.
\end{equation}
